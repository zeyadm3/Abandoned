# Prompt: ABANDONED 0.13.0 — rebuild the Meridian Mall for Early Access

Paste everything below the line into the model. It assumes the model works directly in the repository at
`~/Documents/Abandoned` with shell, file and Blender access.

---

You are the lead environment artist and level designer on **ABANDONED**, a 1–4 player co-op first-person salvage
horror-comedy for Steam, made in Unity 6.3 LTS (URP, C#, Netcode for GameObjects) by a solo developer. Players carry
physical loot out of collapsing abandoned buildings while avoiding monsters, and load it into a truck before it leaves.
The headline feature is the **structural system**: every floor, walkway and stair section has a load limit, cracks
under weight and impacts, and collapses.

The game's only Early Access map is the **Meridian Mall**. Today it's a greybox-quality build with a low-poly kit. Your
job for version **0.13.0** is to **redesign and rebuild the mall from scratch** so it looks and feels like a real
abandoned shopping mall, ready to sell on Steam. It's the map players will see in the trailer, the store screenshots and
their first hour.

## 1. Hard rules (do not break these)

- **Locked stack:** Unity 6.3 LTS, URP, C#, NGO, Unity Input System, Cinemachine, AI Navigation. **No new Unity
  packages, Asset Store assets or paid packs.** ProBuilder is already in the project if you need it.
- **Art sources:** your own original work (Blender via `Tools/Blender/`, procedural textures), plus **CC0 only**
  textures and models (Poly Haven, ambientCG, Kenney, Quaternius, Share Textures or similar). Record **every** third-party
  file in `Docs/ASSET_CREDITS.md` (name, author, licence, URL, what it's used for, where it lives). Third-party files go
  under `Art/ThirdParty/<Source>/<Pack>/`; your own go under `Art/Custom/Environment/` or `Art/Generated/`.
- **This version replaces the "one low-poly style" rule for the environment** (the user's decision for 0.13.0): the mall
  becomes realistic. Write that into the CLAUDE.md decisions log. Characters, monsters and loot keep their current
  look unless they clash badly; flag any that do instead of redoing them.
- **Texture budget ("1080p textures"):** 2048×2048 for hero surfaces (atrium floor, main walls, storefronts, escalators,
  ceilings), 1024×1024 for everything else, 512 for small props and decals. PBR maps: base colour, normal, and a packed
  mask (metallic/occlusion/smoothness for URP Lit). Normal maps imported as normal maps; set Max Size per platform; use
  compression (BC7/DXT5 on desktop); turn on mipmaps. No 4K textures.
- **Never hand-edit `.unity` or `.prefab` YAML.** The mall is generated by Editor builders (`Tools/unity.sh
  rebuild-horror`). Scene content must come from code and data, so the next rebuild reproduces it exactly.
- **Never touch `Scenes/TestMap.unity` or `TestMapBuilder`.** `rebuild-horror` is the command to use; it never
  rebuilds TestMap. Don't run `Tools/unity.sh all` or `rebuild` (they regenerate TestMap and run tests).
- **The HQ isn't part of this job.** If a shared builder change would alter the HQ, keep the HQ looking exactly as it
  does now (the user's hand edits are ported into `HqDressingBuilder`).
- **Testing policy:** the user tests everything. Don't run EditMode/PlayMode tests, nettests or screenshot tests.
  You may run `Tools/unity.sh compile`, `Tools/unity.sh rebuild-horror` and `Tools/unity.sh build`, and you may render
  your own still images of the scene from an Editor script for visual checking (that isn't testing).
  Batch Unity commands need the editor closed.
- **Code style:** one class per file, namespaces `Abandoned.<Area>`, `[SerializeField] private` fields, tunables in
  ScriptableObjects, nothing over ~300 lines (split into partial classes or helpers), comment why not what.
- Commit in logical steps with clear messages ending in `Co-Authored-By:` your attribution. Don't push unless asked.

## 2. What the map must keep doing (gameplay contracts)

The visuals are yours to reinvent. These systems are not; the new mall has to feed them all, or you change the code
that reads them in the same commit.

- **Structural sections.** Every walkable floor piece, walkway, bridge and stair/escalator flight is its own
  `StructuralSection` with a collider, a pivot on the walking surface, the visual as a child, and pre-fractured break
  pieces for the collapse (debris on the Debris layer, cosmetic only). The current grid is 4 m tiles × 3 stories of 4 m.
  You may change the grid, story height and layout, but sections must stay modular and readable: a player should be
  able to see where one floor section ends. Keep per-section "can collapse" flags (ground slabs over solid ground never
  collapse; the basement is real).
- **Visible damage stages.** Sections show stress through stages (sound plus visuals: cracks, sag, dust, then collapse).
  Build crack/sag variants per floor type (terrazzo, carpet, tile, concrete, walkway) that read clearly in the dark.
- **One route that never collapses** (the service stairwell), and authored fallback exits (rope windows, the loading
  bay) so no collapse can trap a crew (GDD 6.4). Keep `MallLayout.Exterior`-style exit data.
- **Doors and shutters.** Store roller shutters (`RollerShutter`) on single-door stores and the front entrance,
  with a store `room` bound each. Stores holding a fallback exit are never shuttered.
- **NavMesh.** Baked once in the builder; collapses carve it (`SectionNavCarver`); flights must be climbable by
  players and monsters (`MallFlightValidator` checks slope, lip, headroom and a NavMesh path).
- **Loot points** tagged by store kind (electronics, clothing, jewelry, furniture, toys, food, office, stock, cinema,
  gallery, concourse, walkway) plus heavy spots and **jackpot points**: the gallery statue behind weak structure on
  the top floor, a heavy furniture piece upstairs, a basement/stock jackpot, and the grand piano on the weak atrium
  bridge. Loot data lives in `LootDefinition.SpawnTags`; don't rename tags without updating the data.
- **Threat spawn points** spread through the building (the director picks one far from and out of sight of players),
  including the basement.
- **The truck** parks at the loading bay's exterior door; player spawns are in the parking lot facing the entrance.
  Keep `MallPopulator.TruckDoorZ` / spawn data in sync with whatever you build.
- **Lights.** Realtime only (power-off runs and collapses mean no lightmap bake). Ceiling `LightFixture`s hang from
  the slab above, fall with it, follow power, and lose power sector by sector as danger rises (`HorrorConfig.SectorOf`;
  two sectors stay lit at max danger). The final phase turns them red. Emergency/alarm lights are named `HorrorAlarm_*`.
- **Horror pacing data.** `Data/Extraction/HorrorConfig.asset` holds the building bounds, the arrival figure and door
  positions, the roar point and the light-sector cell size. Update it to the new layout. Same for
  `BuildingInteriorAtmosphere` bounds, fog and the global volume (`Editor/Art/LevelAtmosphere`).
- **Occlusion.** Baked, but only never-breaking geometry occludes; floors carry no static flags.
- **Network rules.** Level objects that are network objects are spawned dynamically; scene-placed network objects
  must be stamped (`NetworkObjectIds`). The scene must stay in `BuildScenes`.
- **World text** (signs, store names, notices) uses `WorldTextMaterial` (the Abandoned/WorldText shader), never a
  font's GUI material.
- **Rebuild checks must stay at zero:** `[MallValidation]` flights and invisible colliders, and `[MaterialAudit]`
  (every material URP and textured). Every solid collider a player can touch sits inside visible geometry.

## 3. The redesign: what "realistic abandoned mall" means here

Design it as a real 1980s–90s regional mall that closed around 2009 and has stood empty for 15 years: a three-level
enclosed mall (plus a flooded service basement) with an anchor-store frontage, a central skylit atrium with escalators
and a bridge, a food court, a cinema, offices and back-of-house corridors. Use real mall architecture as reference:
terrazzo floors with brass divider strips, planters, fountain basins, glass balustrades with steel handrails, coffered
or drop ceilings with missing tiles, bulkhead signage, roll-down grilles, storefront glazing and display windows,
kiosks, mall directories, escalators with stainless side panels, service corridors with painted block walls and
exposed conduit, a loading dock with dock levellers.

**Make it look abandoned in a believable way, not random.** Decay follows cause:
- Water: the skylight leaks, so the atrium floor has standing water, algae, warped wood, rusted escalator steps,
  stained ceiling tiles and mould climbing walls under the leaks. The basement is flooded.
- Time: dust and grime gradients (heavier at floor edges and corners), faded and sun-bleached signage near windows,
  peeling paint, rust streaks under metal fittings.
- People: scavengers and urban explorers went through first. Smashed display cases, shopping carts, graffiti tags
  (original, non-offensive, no real brands), mattresses and campfire scorch in one corner, boarded windows.
- Nature: plants in skylit areas, leaves blown in by broken doors, birds' droppings under the skylight frame.
- Collapse history: one wing already partly collapsed (the gallery) as an authored wound, with rubble and exposed
  rebar, so players see what the structural system will do before it happens to them.

**Keep the game's aesthetic.** ABANDONED is horror-comedy: dark, tense, dusty and grim, but readable and a bit funny
(silly store names, dark-humour notices, the boss's company-speak). Grounded realism, not photoreal gore. Desaturated
greens, greys and browns with warm sodium/amber and cold fluorescent accents; deep shadows a flashlight cuts through.
Store names and brands are original and fictional (no real trademarks); keep the existing ones' tone (Dead Circuit,
Threadbare, Toy Archive and so on).

**Readability is a gameplay requirement.** In the dark with a flashlight, players must be able to tell:
walkable floor from void; which floor section they're standing on; weak or cracking sections; stairs and escalators
from walls; doors and exits; loot from set dressing (loot keeps its own models and scan tags; set dressing must not look
like pick-up-able loot, and nothing decorative should have a collider a player can snag on). Keep the floor plan
learnable: a crew that played it five times should navigate by landmarks (the fountain, the cinema marquee, the food
court sign, the broken escalator).

**Scale and play space.** Keep roughly the current footprint (about 56 × 48 m, 3 floors + basement) unless the plan
justifies more. Corridors wide enough for a 3-person carry of a piano (4 m+), doors wide enough for Heavy items where
the loot needs to come out, ramps or flat routes from every store to the truck. A run lasts 10–18 minutes.

## 4. Technical approach

- **Modular kit in Blender** (extend `Tools/Blender/environment.py` or add a new script beside it): floor slabs per
  material with their pre-fractured break variants and crack/sag stages, walls, storefront modules (glazed, boarded,
  shuttered), columns, balustrades, ceilings, escalators and stairs, skylight, signage frames, kiosks, planters,
  fountain, food court counters, cinema doors and marquee, back-of-house pieces, loading dock, exterior facade and
  parking lot. Metric units, pivots at the floor/base centre, consistent axes (mind the 0.12.2 lesson in CLAUDE.md:
  Unity (x,y,z) maps to Blender (x,z,y)). Export FBX with stable GUIDs/paths.
- **Materials:** URP Lit, textured, using trim sheets and tiling materials so most of the mall shares a few atlases.
  Add vertex-colour or decal-based grime, leaks and moss so repetition breaks up without hundreds of unique
  textures. Use URP decals (already available in URP) for stains, cracks, graffiti and puddles where it's cheaper.
- **Builders own the scene.** Rewrite the Mall builders (keep them small and split by area) so `rebuild-horror`
  produces the whole map deterministically from layout data plus your kit. Keep `MallLayout` (or its successor) as the
  single source of truth that the populator, loot points, shutters, lights and validators read.
- **Lighting:** realtime only. Fixture density and shadows within budget: few shadow-casting lights, light probes or
  APV if it helps (no baked lightmaps), reflection probes per space, volumetric fog via the existing
  atmosphere/volume (`Editor/Art/LevelAtmosphere`), a moonlit or overcast skylight. Make power-on runs look like failing fluorescents and power-off
  runs genuinely dark.
- **Performance budget:** 60 FPS at 1080p on a mid-range PC as host in the atrium with four flashlights on. Target
  under ~2,500 draw calls/batches after SRP batching and GPU instancing, LODs on everything big (LOD0/1/2 + cull),
  static batching only on never-breaking geometry, occlusion culling, texture memory for the mall under ~1.5 GB.
  Report the numbers from the F1 performance line (or the profiler) in your notes.
- **Audio hooks:** surface tags (`SurfaceTag`, `SurfaceMaterial`) on every walkable and hittable surface so
  footsteps and impacts sound right (terrazzo, carpet, tile, metal, wood, water). Area ambience zones per space.

## 7. How to work

1. **Plan first, then wait.** Present: the concept (one paragraph and a mood list), the floor plans, the zone list with
   each store's story and loot tags, the route and collapse design (where the weak floors, bridge, escalators and the
   pre-collapsed wing are, and why), the art kit list with texture sizes and sources, the lighting plan, the
   performance plan, and the build steps. Ask the user to approve or change it before building.
2. Build in steps, each one compiling and passing `rebuild-horror`'s checks before the next: (a) layout and greybox
   with every gameplay hook working, (b) architecture kit, (c) materials and textures, (d) dressing and decay,
   (e) lighting and atmosphere, (f) performance pass, (g) renders, builds and notes. Commit after each step.
3. If something fails twice, stop and explain the likely causes instead of guessing.
4. When you finish, give the user: what was built, the render folder, the build zip paths, the performance numbers,
   anything you couldn't do, and a numbered list of what to test first in-game.
