## 3. Bugs and risks

### Critical

**B-01 Shutter slam traps players (soft-lock).** Confirmed in code.
- Where: `Extraction/RunHorrorDirector.cs:110-113`, `Extraction/Shutters/RunShutters.cs:96-104` and `:123-130`, `Editor/Mall/MallShutters.cs:20-28`.
- What happens: from danger level 2, each scare tick slams a random open store shutter. The only safety check is "no player within 2 m of the shutter". A player deeper inside the store is locked in. Opening a shutter needs bolt cutters or a crowbar; with neither, pressing E just says "Locked". The six shuttered stores (Threadbare, Jewelry, Security, Toy Archive, Quarantine, Offices) are the single-door ones, so there is no other exit.
- Also: loot inside is locked away, and a shared-carried item sitting in the doorway (carriers more than 2 m away) gets the shutter's collider switched on inside it.
- To reproduce: solo, no crowbar, stand at the back of Jewelry from about minute 4 and wait.
- Fix direction: never slam a shutter whose room contains a living player or a carried item; or let any shutter be lifted slowly by hand from the inside.

**B-02 Debug and cheat keys are live in every build.** Confirmed in code.
- Where: `Core/DebugViewToggle.cs` (placed in HQ and Mall by the builders), `Player/PlayerInputReader.cs:29-39`, `Player/PlayerRagdoll.cs:148-153`, `UI/Menus/MenuUi.cs:206-208`, `Player/FreeCamera.cs`.
- What happens: F1 shows the debug overlay to anyone (monster names and states drawn through walls, structure health, danger, seeds). K toggles the ragdoll: instant get-up after a knockdown, and it cancels fall damage (see B-10). F9 gives any player a free-flying camera to scout the whole mall. F10 hides the HUD. Only F2, minus and equals are limited to development builds (`Structure/StructureDebugControls.cs:47`).
- Fix direction: gate all of them behind the Dev build flavour (or a host-only setting); keep F10 if wanted as a photo feature.

### High

**B-03 Armored Bay upgrade has no effect.** Confirmed in code.
- Where: `Extraction/RunState.cs:38` (`Shelters`, never called anywhere), `Threats/Threat.cs:70` (`Sheltered`).
- What happens: every monster already skips players standing in the truck, with or without the upgrade. The $40,000 level-9 purchase changes nothing.
- Related design problem: the truck is a free, permanent safe room from every monster (see D-04).

**B-04 Level travel has no timeout.** Confirmed in code.
- Where: `Networking/SessionTravel.cs:44-53`, `Extraction/RunDirector.cs:95`.
- What happens: the host waits until every connected client reports the level loaded. A client that hangs without disconnecting stalls the whole crew: the run never starts and nobody is placed. There is no "waiting for player X" message and no way for the host to continue.

**B-05 Levels load synchronously.** Confirmed in code; size needs playtest.
- Where: `Networking/SessionTravel.cs:126`.
- What happens: `LoadScene` blocks each machine for the whole load. The travel screen cannot animate, and a long stall on a slow disk can outlast the network timeout and drop the player.
- Fix direction: async load with the travel screen as a progress view.

**B-06 A crash or lost connection counts as a failed job.** Confirmed in code.
- Where: `Company/CompanyLedger.cs:39-47`, `Company/CompanyService.cs:108-112` and `:233-236`.
- What happens: the quota is written to the save when the van leaves. If the host quits, crashes or loses Steam mid-job, the next launch settles it as an empty haul: 50 % of the quota as a penalty, running costs, and a missed-quota strike. Three crashes in a row bankrupt the company.
- Fix direction: one forgiven abandon per session, or no strike when the game did not exit cleanly.

**B-07 No real player names.** Confirmed in code.
- Where: `UI/Menus/PauseMenuView.cs:128`, `UI/ChatView.cs:118`, `Extraction/RunState.cs:244` (appraisal screen).
- What happens: everyone is "Player N", where N is the network client id plus one. Client ids keep rising when people rejoin, so a four-player crew can contain "Player 6". Steam names are never shown.

**B-08 Clients can spend company money and start the van.** Confirmed in code.
- Where: `Company/CompanyService.cs:223-224` (van), `:290-291` (gear), `:345-346` (truck upgrades).
- What happens: these requests are accepted from any connected player with no check. Only choosing the job is host-only. One player can empty the account or drive off while others are still at the gear rack.
- Fix direction: a host option, "crew may spend / crew may drive".

**B-09 Truck departure cannot be cancelled.** Confirmed in code.
- Where: `Extraction/RunState.cs:165-178`.
- What happens: any living player at the lever starts a 10-second honk and the truck leaves, even below quota, even by accident. No confirmation, no cancel, no vote.

### Medium

**B-10 Fall damage can be dodged by ragdolling mid-air.** Confirmed in code.
- Where: `Player/PlayerRagdoll.cs:230` (fall height restarts when the ragdoll begins), `Networking/NetworkPlayer.Health.cs:96-102` (the host only ever lowers the reported height).
- What happens: becoming a ragdoll during a fall (K key, or being hit by thrown loot) measures the fall from that moment, not from the top. A 12 m fall can cost nothing.

**B-11 Cannot press E on anything while holding loot.** Confirmed in code.
- Where: `Interaction/PlayerInteractor.cs:64-70`.
- What happens: with any item in hand, the truck lever, shutters, van, terminal and notes cannot be used, and small loot cannot be pocketed (the pickup rules at `Interaction/PickupRules.cs:35-40` would allow it). Players must drop a laptop to pull a lever.

**B-12 A dead teammate's visible body may not be where the medkit works.** Confirmed in code; frequency needs playtest.
- Where: `Player/PlayerCorpsePresentation.cs:27`, `Equipment/PlayerEquipment.cs:320-337`.
- What happens: other players see a copy of the corpse that starts at the dead player's standing position and falls on its own. The medkit measures 2.5 m to the real body position. After a knockback or a long tumble the two differ, so the medkit "does nothing" over the visible body.

**B-13 Monsters can appear next to players.** Confirmed in code.
- Where: `Threats/ThreatDirector.cs:113-119`.
- What happens: the spawn point is chosen by seed and count only. There is no minimum distance or line-of-sight check.

**B-14 The Stalker breathes with the building's creak sound.** Confirmed in code.
- Where: `Threats/Stalker.cs:131-137`.
- What happens: it plays the structural creak every 2.2 seconds. The game's core rule is that players learn what a creak means; this teaches the wrong thing.

**B-15 Long non-English chat lines can fail.** Confirmed in code; exact failure needs a test.
- Where: `Networking/NetworkChat.cs:32-33`, `UI/ChatView.cs:119`.
- What happens: text is cut to 120 characters, then stored in a 125-byte container. Arabic, Chinese or emoji use 2–4 bytes per character, so a line over about 60 characters overflows (an error in development builds, cut text otherwise). Typing `</noparse>` also lets a player inject formatting tags. The UI fonts are Latin-only, so such text may show as boxes anyway.

**B-16 Pocket handling is limited.** Confirmed in code.
- Where: `Interaction/PlayerInteractor.cs:76`, `Interaction/PlayerCarrier.cs:342-347`.
- What happens: dropping from a pocket needs Tab + right mouse with empty hands, and always drops the last item picked up. There is no way to choose an item.

**B-17 Dead player's pocket loot spawns in one spot.** Confirmed in code; effect needs playtest.
- Where: `Networking/Loot/LootServerActions.cs:114-126`, `Networking/NetworkPlayer.cs:219-220`.
- What happens: every pocketed item is released in the same frame at the same point, one metre above where the player stood (not where the body lies). Overlapping items can be flung apart and damaged.

**B-18 Sealed jobs do not warn about tools.** Confirmed in code.
- Where: `Extraction/Shutters/RunShutters.cs:64-67`, `Company/ContractBoardScreen.cs:68-79`.
- What happens: a Sealed job shutters the front entrance and every store. The board never says bolt cutters or a crowbar are needed, and the starter kit has neither. Whether the loading bay still lets a crew in needs a playtest.

**B-19 Held loot may not count at extraction.** Confirmed in code.
- Where: `Extraction/TruckCargo.cs:36-53`, `Extraction/RunState.cs:226-246`.
- What happens: pocketed loot counts if its owner is in the truck's ride zone. Loot held in hands counts only if its centre is inside the smaller cargo-bay box. A player standing in the truck holding a painting can lose it.

**B-20 A total wipe still pays in full.** Confirmed in code; a design call.
- Where: `Extraction/RunState.cs:138`, `Company/CompanyLedger.cs:13-32`.
- What happens: when everyone is dead the truck "leaves" with whatever is in the bay and the crew is paid the full haul, bonus included. Dying costs only pocket loot.

**B-21 Runtime shader lookup on the truck.** Confirmed in code; build result needs a check.
- Where: `Extraction/TruckSanctuary.cs:30` and `:40`.
- What happens: `Shader.Find` returns nothing in a build if the shader was stripped. Only two materials in the project use URP Unlit. If neither ships, the rear-view mirror turns pink and the truck's setup stops halfway.

**B-22 A throw can be silently swallowed.** Confirmed in code.
- Where: `Networking/Loot/NetworkLoot.cs:204-208`.
- What happens: on a client, a throw sent while an earlier release request is still pending is dropped without feedback, and the charge resets.

**B-23 Pickup aim is blocked by debris and other players.** Confirmed in code.
- Where: `Interaction/PlayerInteractor.cs:152-168`.
- What happens: the nearest thing on the aim ray wins, on every layer. Rubble from a collapse in front of loot blocks picking it up until the rubble despawns.

**B-24 Missing session controls.** Confirmed by search.
- No kick or ban. No company rename (the name is fixed as "Zeyad Salvage Co.", `Company/CompanySave.cs:17`). No "new company" or "delete save" outside the demo. One save slot.

**B-25 Per-player voice volume is forgotten.** Confirmed in code.
- Where: `Voice/VoiceSettings.cs:31-49`. Stored for the session by client id, so it resets on every rejoin.

## 4. Balance and design concerns

All need playtest numbers; the code and data make each one likely.

- **D-01 Light.** Flashlight battery is 180 seconds per job with no recharge (`Data/Equipment/FlashlightConfig`). Jobs last 10–18 minutes. Building lights die one sector per danger level (one level every 110 seconds) and are all dead at level 6, which arrives at minute 11, or the moment the window closes on a 10-minute job. A spare cell costs $500, is single use and needs the second hand slot. Expect most of every job to be played in the dark.
- **D-02 Structure ageing.** From danger 1, every 9 seconds as many sections as the danger level lose 10 % health down to 30 %. At danger 3 that is about 20 sections a minute out of roughly 300, on top of 75 % faster overload decay.
- **D-03 Final phase.** 90 seconds after the window closes the Last Hunter spawns and kills on contact. Combined with D-01 this is very punishing for solo players.
- **D-04 Truck camping.** No monster can hurt anyone inside the truck (B-03). A crew can wait there for free.
- **D-05 Money.** Any haul pays, a wipe pays (B-20), and running costs are $6,000 plus $1,200 per level. Check whether money still piles up by level 5 as BALANCE.md asks.
- **D-06 No plain jobs.** With three or more modifiers unlocked every offer carries one (`Contracts/ContractGenerator.cs:21-32`), and six are unlocked at level 1. New players never see an unmodified mall.
- **D-07 Blind One.** It damages anyone within reach even when they are silent. Check that crouch-walking past it is still possible, as the design promises.
- **D-08 Collector.** Stolen loot is hidden at a random point with no clue where. Likely to feel like loot simply vanished.
- **D-09 Carry rules.** Solo Heavy dragging needs the hand trolley equipped in one of two hand slots, leaving one slot for a flashlight and none for a radio, medkit or tool.
- **D-10 Gear is fixed for the whole job.** Gear cannot be dropped or handed over. A dead player's medkit or bolt cutters are lost to the crew.

## 5. Performance risks

None measured. Check each with F1's performance line in the mall as host.

- **P-01** The host recomputes structural load every physics step: every resting loot item (about 86), player and monster fires 5 raycasts, roughly 450 raycasts 50 times a second (`Structure/StructureSimulation.cs:188-211`). Resting items could be cached.
- **P-02** The Collector's search runs a physics overlap of the truck bay once per loot item, about 86 overlaps in one frame, on every theft attempt and every 3 seconds when it finds nothing (`Threats/Collector.cs:125-133`).
- **P-03** The horror director builds a new player list every frame on the host and updates every light fixture every frame on every machine (`Extraction/RunHorrorDirector.cs:73`, `:117-130`). At run start it walks every untagged object in the scene looking for alarm lights (`:35`).
- **P-04** Hunter and Stalker request a new path every frame (`Threats/Hunter.cs:94`, `Threats/Stalker.cs:82-87`). The Blind One already limits this to four times a second.
- **P-05** The Weight and the Last Hunter search the whole scene for structural sections every few seconds (`Threats/TheWeight.cs:38`, `Threats/LastHunter.cs:41`).
- **P-06** Each dead player with a lit flashlight leaves a full copy of the flashlight light on the corpse (`Player/PlayerCorpsePresentation.cs:48`). The truck adds a second camera and render texture while someone stands in it (`Extraction/TruckSanctuary.cs:32-36`).
- **P-07** All lighting is realtime. Four shadow-casting flashlights plus ceiling fixtures in the atrium is the worst case for the 60 fps target.
- **P-08** About 20 components define `OnGUI` debug drawing and still receive GUI events in release builds. Small, but free to remove with B-02.

## 6. Code health and rule breaks

- **C-01 Code style.** Four files are written with no spaces and several statements per line, against the project's style rules: `Extraction/RunHorrorDirector.cs`, `Extraction/TruckSanctuary.cs`, `Interaction/MysteryNote.cs`, and `ServerSlam` in `Extraction/Shutters/RunShutters.cs`.
- **C-02 Not data-driven.** The horror director only works in a scene named "Mall" and contains fixed world coordinates (`Extraction/RunHorrorDirector.cs:32`, `:60`, `:81`, `:107`, `:141`). A second level would need code changes.
- **C-03 Size rule.** Ten hand-written files exceed about 300 lines: NetworkBootstrap 439, PlayerEquipment 420, CompanyService 398, PlayerCarrier 394, MenuUi 334, NetworkLoot 324, SharedCarryable 321, StructureSimulation 315, TestMapBuilder 308, LootTags 305.
- **C-04 Tunables in code.** Many numbers that should be in config assets are literals: monster damage fallbacks, crawler flashlight cone and range, shutter slam distance, scare timings, heartbeat rates, truck lamp values.
- **C-05 Settings storage is inconsistent.** The microphone device uses PlayerPrefs directly while everything else uses `Core.Prefs` (`Voice/VoiceSettings.cs:55-59`).
- **C-06 Tests are probably stale.** About 480 test cases exist, but none have run since M10. MallTests coordinates were edited without running.
- **C-07 Steam is unproven.** Lobbies, invites, Steam voice, achievements hand-off and cloud saves have only run against a fake Steam client.

Good news from the read: host authority is applied consistently, client requests are validated on the host (reach, ownership, speed clamps, rate limits), the save file is written safely with a corrupt-file backup, and static state is reset correctly between sessions.

## 8. New feature ideas

Ranked by value for the effort.

**Communication and co-op**
1. Ping / marker (middle mouse or a key): mark loot, a weak floor, a monster. Essential for players without a microphone.
2. Steam names over heads and in every list (fixes B-07).
3. Cancel window and "quota not met" warning on the truck lever (fixes B-09).
4. Hand gear to a teammate and loot gear from a body (fixes D-10).
5. Ghost abilities: dead players can flicker a light or knock once to warn the living.
6. Emotes (point, wave, panic), cheap and good for clips.

**Light and survival**
7. A breaker room: restore one sector's lights for a few minutes, loudly. Gives the crew an answer to D-01.
8. Glow sticks or flares: cheap, throwable, short-lived light.
9. Recharge the flashlight at the truck.
10. Closable doors and lockers to hide in, completing the Hunter's counterplay.

**Loot and economy**
11. Collector nest: stolen loot gathers in one findable place.
12. Boss requests: "bring me a piano" for a bonus, on top of the quota.
13. Loot bag or crate: carry several small items as one fragile load.
14. Company naming, logo on the van, truck paint.
15. Trophy shelf at the HQ for jackpots sold; a loot catalogue that fills in as items are found.
16. Bestiary: each monster's rule is written down once the crew has survived it.

**Structure of play**
17. A tutorial job: one small scripted run that teaches carry, weight, cracks and the truck.
18. Daily seeded contract with a leaderboard: same mall, same loot for everyone.
19. Host difficulty rules (see section 10).
20. Photo mode as a real feature (F9 is already most of it), limited to dead players or the HQ.
21. Post-run highlights: biggest drop, longest fall, who broke what.
22. Save slots, and rejoining at the HQ after a disconnect.
23. Steam rich presence ("In the mall, $32k of $40k").

## 10. More options for the player

What exists today: field of view, head bob, camera shake, crosshair, UI scale, tips, mouse sensitivity,
invert Y, crouch hold/toggle, key rebinding, window mode, resolution, VSync, frame cap, brightness,
quality preset, shadows, render scale, anti-aliasing, four volume sliders, mute in background, voice
volume, microphone picker, push-to-talk or open mic, mute microphone, subtitles and size, colourblind
scanner, reduce menu effects.

**Accessibility (do these first)**
- **O-01** Reduce flashing lights in the game itself: fixture flicker, flashlight flicker near monsters, alarm lights, scares. The current switch only covers menus.
- Subtitles on by default, with speaker labels and a background.
- Arachnophobia mode for the Crawlers.
- Reduce jump scares (the cosmetic apparitions).
- Camera shake and head bob as sliders, not on/off.
- Turn off the low-health blur and vignette.
- Colourblind-safe loot tags and warnings, not only the scanner.
- Hold or toggle for sprint, push-to-talk and radio.
- Heartbeat and breathing volume.

**Controls**
- Controller support with sensitivity, dead zone and invert.
- Separate horizontal and vertical sensitivity.
- Rebindable chat, pause, photo keys; a second binding per action.
- Mouse wheel to switch hand slots or pockets.
- Choose which pocket item to drop.

**Audio and voice**
- Microphone gain and an open-mic threshold.
- Output device.
- Separate radio volume.
- Turn voice chat off entirely; text chat off; a chat filter.
- Remember each friend's voice volume by Steam id.

**Video**
- Monitor picker.
- Toggles for fog quality, bloom, film grain and post-processing.
- Texture quality and light count.
- An FPS counter.
- Field of view above 100 for ultrawide screens.

**Interface**
- HUD opacity and a minimal HUD.
- Damage numbers on or off.
- Streamer mode that hides IP addresses, lobby ids and names.
- Language (no localisation exists; all text is in code).

**Host rules for a session**
- Difficulty preset: relaxed, standard, brutal.
- Window length, monster count, structure fragility, flashlight battery.
- Crew may spend money / crew may start the van / crew may pull the truck lever.
- Friendly knockdowns on or off.
- Kick a player.
- Lobby privacy: friends only or invite only.

**Save and profile**
- Company name.
- New company and delete save.
- Save slots.
- Statistics page.
